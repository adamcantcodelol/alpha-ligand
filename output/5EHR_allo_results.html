What this is

A protein does its job using a small pocket on its surface — the active site. If you can plug that pocket with a small molecule, you switch the protein off. That molecule is a repressor, and finding one is what this pipeline is for.

The computer did three things. First it measured the pocket of 5EHR_allo around the residues you named (TA:108, EA:110, RA:111, WA:112, FA:113, HA:114, TA:218, TA:219, EA:249, EA:250, TA:253, LA:254, QA:257, DA:489, PA:491, KA:492, QA:495, YA:511) and wrote down what chemistry the pocket is asking for — charges, flat rings, greasy patches. Then it assembled small molecules that carry that chemistry. Finally it used AutoDock Vina to physically place each molecule inside the pocket and score how well it fits. Those scores are the ranking below.

The one-line summary: this is a shortlist worth testing at the bench, not a discovery. Most molecules that score well here do not bind in real life. That is normal, and it is why you test a handful rather than one.

How to read this

  • Docking score (kcal/mol). How well Vina thinks the molecule sits in the pocket. More negative is better. −9 is a good fit, −5 is a poor one. Differences smaller than about 1 kcal/mol are inside the noise — treat those molecules as tied, not ranked. Anything tied for the top is flagged below.
  • Chemistry checklist (0–100). Purely how much of the pocket's wish list the molecule's chemistry covers. It is not an affinity and not a docking score. A molecule can score 95 here and still be far too bulky to get in.
  • MW is molecular weight — how big it is. cLogP is greasiness: high means oily, low means water-loving; roughly 0–3 is comfortable for a drug-like molecule. H-bond donors/acceptors count the places it can hydrogen-bond to the protein. Rotatable bonds is how floppy it is; floppier molecules pay a bigger penalty on binding, so fewer is usually better.
  • SMILES is the molecule written as a line of text. Copy it and paste it into the search box at a chemical vendor (Enamine, Mcule, Sigma-Aldrich) to see whether that molecule, or something close to it, is buyable.

Want to look at the 3D poses? Open the _docked.sdf file alongside the protein structure in a free viewer such as ChimeraX or PyMOL.

What these numbers are not

No measured affinity appears anywhere in this report. There is no Kd, Ki or IC50 here, because none has been measured. Anyone who quotes you one from a docking run is guessing.
  • A docking score is an empirical estimate, not a measurement. Vina's scoring function is fitted to known structures and correlates only loosely with real binding strength. Treat it as accurate to roughly ±2–3 kcal/mol.
  • A good score is not a validated binder. It means the molecule can be placed in the pocket without clashing, in a way the scoring function likes. Plenty of molecules that do that never bind.
  • The protein was held rigid. Real side chains move to accommodate a ligand, and whole loops can shift. A molecule rejected here might fit a protein that is allowed to flex, and vice versa.
  • Water was stripped out. Crystal waters often bridge a ligand to the protein; removing them is standard practice but it throws away real interactions.
  • One protonation state was assumed. Charges on both the ligand and the protein were assigned by rule at pH 7, and getting one wrong changes the score.
  • Only the pocket you named was searched. If the residues were wrong, everything here describes the wrong hole in the protein.
  • Small differences mean nothing. Inside about 1 kcal/mol the ordering is noise.

So what do I actually do with this?

  1. Read the ranked list and pick the top few — including the tied ones, not just #1.
  2. Copy each SMILES into a vendor search (Enamine, Mcule, Sigma-Aldrich) and see what is buyable, or close enough to buyable.
  3. Open the poses in ChimeraX or PyMOL and sanity-check them by eye: is the charged group actually near the residue it is supposed to pair with?
  4. Order a handful and assay them against your protein. The bench is the only thing here that decides.
  5. If nothing is buyable, the pocket's shopping list above is still useful on its own — it tells you what chemistry to look for in a compound library you already have.
