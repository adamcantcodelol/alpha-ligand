Read this first

This is a composition-based design, not a structure-based one. The pocket profile said which chemistry a binder should present; these sequences present it, in roughly the right proportions, at a sensible length. The method never folded a peptide, never placed a residue in the pocket, and produced no affinity estimate. There is no Kd here because none was calculated.

Real structure-based mini-protein binder design uses RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN, Rosetta and AlphaFold2/AF-Multimer, which build an actual 3D interface and score it. Treat this list as a cheap first panel to express and screen, and expect most of it to do nothing — that is the normal outcome for a composition-only method, not a failure of your bench work.

Small molecule or peptide?

  • Small molecule (the other branch of this project): you search a vendor catalogue and buy it. Fast, but you can only buy what exists.
  • Peptide (this page): you express it in E. coli from the plasmid insert plasmid_builder.py designs, or order it synthesised. Slower, but any sequence is makeable.

What to do with this at the bench

  1. Pick a few, not one. Coverage differences of a point or two mean nothing. Taking the top 3–5 across different charge/GRAVY values gives you a better panel than taking the single top row.
  2. Decide how to make them. Either order them as synthetic peptides (fast, costs money per peptide, no cloning) or express them from the insert in the .gb files (cheap per peptide once cloned, needs an NdeI/XhoI vector such as a pET backbone, and purification off the His tag).
  3. Screen for binding, do not assume it. A pull-down on the His tag, fluorescence polarisation, BLI/SPR or a simple thermal shift of your target are all reasonable first assays. Include a scrambled version of the same composition as your negative control — that is the control that tests whether the order of residues matters, which is exactly what this method does not model.
  4. If nothing binds, that is information. It is the expected result often enough that a negative panel should push you toward structure-based design (RFdiffusion/ProteinMPNN) rather than toward more composition sampling.

Honest limitations

  • No folding, no docking, no 3D structure, no energy calculation.
  • No affinity, Kd, IC50 or probability of binding is computed or implied anywhere.
  • Residue ORDER is essentially arbitrary: the score only sees composition, so two sequences with the same residues score identically however they are arranged.
  • A peptide may not be able to reach a buried pocket at all. Pockets that fit a small molecule are often too enclosed for a peptide backbone.
  • Solubility, aggregation, protease sensitivity and cell permeability are not modelled. GRAVY and the instability index are crude proxies only.
  • Cys is excluded by default, so disulfide-stapled or cyclic designs are out of scope here.
  • The pocket profile itself came from one crystal structure and the residues you named. Wrong residues in, wrong peptides out.
