3. WHAT THE SCORE MEANS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The checklist score is built from exactly two things:
  (a) 70% - chemistry match. For each feature class the pocket asked
      for (cationic, aromatic, hydrophobic, ...), does this molecule
      contain a group of that kind? Found via RDKit SMARTS pattern
      matching. Having one counts for most of the credit; extra
      copies add a little more, with diminishing returns.
  (b) 30% - property-window fit. The fraction of the five suggested
      windows (MW, cLogP, HBD, HBA, rotatable bonds) the molecule
      falls inside.

The score is therefore a measure of CHEMICAL SHOPPING-LIST COVERAGE.

It is NOT:
  - a binding affinity, Kd, Ki or IC50;
  - a docking score;
  - an interaction energy with the protein;
  - evidence that the molecule fits in the pocket at all.

Nothing in this script ever puts a molecule inside the protein. The
3D coordinates are the molecule's own relaxed shape in a vacuum, not
a pose in the binding site. The geometry of the pharmacophore (the
3D anchor points in the JSON) is NOT used to place atoms - only the
list of feature TYPES is used. Two molecules with identical scores
can differ a thousand-fold in real affinity, and a high-scoring
molecule may be sterically unable to enter the pocket.

The force-field energies are internal strain of the isolated
molecule, useful only for comparing conformers of the SAME molecule.

The candidate library is a small hand-written starter kit (14 scaffolds,
21 substituent groups, 9 known fragments), not a real screening
collection. Many of the assembled molecules may be hard or impossible
to synthesise; no synthetic accessibility check is done.

HONEST NEXT STEP: feed the SDF file to a docking program
(AutoDock Vina or smina) together with the protein structure. That is
the first step that actually tests fit. Even then, docking scores are
weak predictors and only an experiment settles it.
