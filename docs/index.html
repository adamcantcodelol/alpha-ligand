Step 1 of 3 — pick a protein

A protein is a molecule in your cells with a dent in its surface, called a pocket. Drugs work by sitting in that dent. This page looks at a pocket and tells you what kind of molecule would fit it.

To start, we need a 3D picture of your protein. Every solved protein structure in the world has a free four-character ID called a PDB code — for example 3ERT is a breast-cancer protein with the drug tamoxifen stuck in it.

How to read this

Section 4 is a pharmacophore: a list of the chemical groups a good binder should carry, and roughly where. A positively charged wall wants a negatively charged group on the ligand; an aromatic wall wants a ring to stack against; a greasy wall wants a greasy group. Build or search for a molecule that places those groups near those points, then dock and test it.
